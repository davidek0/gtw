## The Hospital at Home (HaH) Market Gap

The foundational technology required for HaH—such as remote monitoring, telemed applications, and advanced portable medical devices—already exists. The primary barriers preventing the scaling of this care model are not technological, but organizational, logistical, and financial. Startups that shift their focus from creating new medical hardware to building the operational and financial infrastructure for decentralized care are uniquely positioned to capture this market.

## High-Potential Startup Opportunities

**1. Unified Stakeholder Coordination & Workflow Hub**

- **The Problem:** Delivering acute care at home requires complex, interprofessional collaboration across regional hospitals, municipal primary care, specialized mobile teams, patients, and their families. Currently, integrating these disparate systems and actors is a major hurdle.

- **The Solution:** A centralized workflow platform designed specifically for decentralized care. It could coordinate scheduling for multidisciplinary teams, manage remote care plans, and provide a unified communication interface for all stakeholders. This would directly address the need for a cohesive care chain and smooth patient transitions between care levels.

**2. Value-Based Financing & KPI Engine for HaH**

- **The Problem:** Current healthcare reimbursement models and structural definitions are rigidly tied to physical hospital beds, which impedes the funding of home-based acute care. Furthermore, evaluating HaH using traditional inpatient KPIs is misleading; the true cost savings of HaH manifest later through reduced hospital readmissions, fewer post-acute rehabilitation stays, and a decrease in hospital-acquired infections.

- **The Solution:** A financial analytics platform that tracks complete, episode-based care costs rather than just daily operational expenses. By quantifying the long-term savings and clinical outcomes of HaH interventions, this tool could provide the necessary data infrastructure for health regions and insurance providers to adopt shared-savings reimbursement models.

**3. Mobile Supply Chain & Logistics Optimizer**

- **The Problem:** Moving hospital-level care to distributed homes introduces significant supply chain inefficiencies. For example, if a mobile care worker forgets a specific needle or compress, they may face a 30-minute drive back to the hospital, severely disrupting care efficiency.

- **The Solution:** A mobile inventory and predictive logistics software for HaH fleets. By utilizing patient data and treatment plans to automatically generate packing lists for mobile care units, this product could prevent missing supplies and optimize routing for care teams.

**4. Patient & Family Onboarding / Empowerment Portal**

- **The Problem:** The success of HaH heavily relies on the willingness and technical capability of patients and their relatives to use digital monitoring equipment and participate in care. However, the system cannot demand that relatives assume excessive medical responsibility.

- **The Solution:** An intuitive onboarding and education platform that gamifies or simplifies the learning process for patients and families. It could offer step-by-step guidance on operating remote diagnostic tools, securely stream patient-generated data (like smart watch metrics) into the formal healthcare system, and provide a direct, low-friction channel to professional support.

## Key Go-to-Market Considerations

To successfully deploy these solutions, a startup must navigate Sweden's fragmented healthcare landscape, which requires strong regional and political cooperation. Additionally, any platform handling patient data or interfacing with medical devices must maintain strict compliance with data privacy regulations and CE-marking requirements, which currently dictate the pace of innovation in the medtech space. Overcoming the deep-seated cultural resistance from medical professionals who are accustomed to a hospital-centric care model will require solutions that emphasize ease-of-use and clearly demonstrate reduced administrative burden.
